NY Food Inspections

Web app Maps Open data

Current health inspection grades for restaurants across New York State, from official public data rather than reviews.

Try it live (opens in a new tab)

The problem

Inspection results are public, but they're buried in health department records as lists of violation codes. Unless a restaurant is somewhere that posts letter grades, there's no quick way to tell how it did. Review sites rate the food and service, not how clean the kitchen is.

What I built

A statewide search and map built only on official inspection data.

  • Search restaurants by name or location, or browse the map
  • Tap a result or map pin to see its current grade
  • View each restaurant's inspection history

Data source

Official health department inspection and violation records.

One interesting decision

Not every health department issues letter grades. If there's an official grade, the app shows it. If not, it calculates one from the violation records using a model based on LA County's letter-grade system, so every restaurant gets a grade on the same scale.

I didn't use New York City's system because its score is built from NYC's own violation codes and point values, which other health departments in the state don't use. Most of the statewide data is just counts of critical and non-critical violations. LA County's system is well known and simpler: start at 100, subtract points for each major and minor violation, then apply letter cutoffs. That works with the counts the data has.

Calculated grades are labeled "Estimated" in search results, and each restaurant's page notes that its grade isn't an official government grade.
